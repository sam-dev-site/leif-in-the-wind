Cypress belongs here because the sources place it here. It does not belong in Newfoundland, the Saint John valley, or the Rice Lake Plains.

## Ecoregion

- Real-world analogue: the **Atchafalaya Basin**, south-central Louisiana, about 30°N. The largest remaining forested wetland in the contiguous United States, and the Mississippi’s main distributary swamp. The river’s name is the Choctaw *hacha falaia*, “long river.”
- Compressed journey: in the game this band sits inland of the Rice Lake grass. The walk is shortened. The miles from Ontario to Louisiana are not tiled. The plants are this swamp’s plants.
- Not the next wet woods south of Rice Lake. Southern Ontario and the Maritimes do not have this cypress. Baldcypress runs the Atlantic coastal plain from southern Delaware to Florida, along the Gulf to southeastern Texas, and up the Mississippi valley to southern Illinois and southwestern Indiana. Water tupelo stops at the southern tip of Illinois. North of that line, a cypress is an ornamental, not this biome.
- Period: 11th century. The cypress and tupelo were already the old swamp. Industrial logging, spoil banks, and the navigation channel are later. Today large parts of the basin stay flooded more than half the year, and backswamps go hypoxic, because dredging and spoil banks cut the old north-to-south flow. That stalled modern swamp is not the picture. The 11th-century swamp still flooded. It was not this logged, blocked basin.
- The basin was already lived in. This file does not design a meeting, a village, or a mound. The land-bridge tide pinned in `docs/IDEAS.md` is not this water. The swamp does not drain because night fell.

## Climate

Humid Gulf coastal plain. Baldcypress country gets roughly 1120 mm of rain a year at the north end of its range and about 1630 mm on the central Gulf. The growing season is long. Winters are mild next to Newfoundland. The game’s night stays the coast rule anyway: same cold, same hearts. Do not invent a warm night, a wet season, or a hurricane.

Ground underfoot, in three kinds:

- **Bayou channel.** Open, slow, meandering water. One of many. Not a single stem across the band the way the Saint John is.
- **Backswamp.** Cypress and tupelo in water that stands or moves through the trees. In sloughs, water tupelo takes the deeper part and baldcypress the margin and the shallower part. In deep still water they share the depth.
- **Higher floodplain.** The ground that floods less. Overcup oak, water hickory, and the other bottomland trees. This is the walk that is not a wade.

No peat bog, no granite, no spruce, no tallgrass hill.

## Water, for the later spawn

Phase J needs a way through water that is not one stem. The sources support three kinds of ground and no width in tiles.

- The **bayou channel** is open water. He does not wade it. There are many channels, lakes, and sloughs, not one wall of river across the band. The raft he already carries can matter on a channel it can cross. It does not drain the swamp.
- The **tupelo slough** is the deeper part of the flooded woods. Treat it as water he does not walk, distinct from the channel only in that trees stand in it.
- The **cypress margin** and the **higher floodplain** are where he walks. Cypress grows on saturated and seasonally inundated soil, shallower than the tupelo slough. The higher oaks and the pecan are the dry walk. Do not turn every water tile into a brook he can cross, and do not turn the whole band into the Saint John stem.
- No tile count is in these sources. Phase J may use the split above. It may not invent a depth, a flood calendar, or a second boat to fill a blank.

## Do not include

- **Baldcypress or Spanish moss on the coast, in the valley, or on the Rice Lake Plains.** Out of range.
- **Pondcypress as the river-swamp tree.**
- **Fir, spruce, peat, granite, bakeapple, blueberry, sugar maple, big bluestem, black oak savanna, New Jersey tea, elk.** Those are earlier bands.
- **Nutria.** Introduced. The Jean Lafitte preserve names it as introduced in south Louisiana swamps. Not an 11th-century animal. The 1982 project text lists it as a modern furbearer.
- **Water hyacinth and hydrilla.** USGS names them as invasive plants that choke the basin. Not the old cover.
- **A year-round hypoxic, spoil-banked swamp** as the 11th-century look. That is the modified basin.
- **Ivory-billed woodpecker, Florida panther, a rookery to manage.** Not established here as something to spawn.
- **Maize, a rice field, a crawfish pond, a canal.** Later uses of this water. Not scatter forage.
- Palms as a fantasy canopy. Buttonbush is the shrub these sources name.
