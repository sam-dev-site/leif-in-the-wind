# Biome research: `inland_plains`

Status: **draft — owner should skim before art or spawn tables**

Research only. Nothing in this file is authorized for `game/`, spawn tables, art filenames, or PixelLab prompts until that skim. `inland_plains.tres` stays empty.

The mission’s wish for this step is mixed grassland, large game, and a mineral that becomes a travel tool. This pass went looking for a real corridor inland of the Saint John valley. The grassland is real. The iron tool is not.

## Ecoregion

- Real-world analogue: the **Rice Lake Plains**, on the east end of the Oak Ridges Moraine, south of Rice Lake, Ontario, about 44°N (Peterborough and Northumberland counties). Tallgrass prairie and black-oak savanna. Alderville First Nation’s Black Oak Savanna is the remnant people can still walk.
- This is the northeastern limit of tallgrass. The high plains, the shortgrass, and a bison horizon are a different country. They are not this band.
- Compressed journey: in the game this band sits inland of the Saint John valley. The walk is shortened. The miles from New Brunswick to Rice Lake are not tiled one by one. The plants are the plants of these hills, not a costume of “prairie” laid on the Madawaska.
- The land immediately inland of the Saint John is the wrong picture. The Madawaska highlands, in the Central Uplands, are cooler forest: balsam fir, red, white, and black spruce, and the tolerant hardwoods sugar maple, yellow birch, and beech. Yellow birch and sugar maple are most abundant in those uplands. That is still the coast’s conifers and the valley’s hardwoods. Spawning it as grassland would invent a biome. It is not `inland_plains`.
- Period: 11th century. The open grass was kept by fire for centuries before Europeans. Farms, hay fields, and the pasture plants that replaced the grass are later. Today less than 1% of Ontario’s tallgrass remains, and less than 3% of these grasslands remain more widely. The game’s ground is the earlier cover, not the remnant and not a restoration planting.
- People kept this open with fire. The later name Pemedashdakota / Pemedashcoutayang, “Lake of the Burning Plains,” is Mississauga. Ojibwe presence at Rice Lake is recorded from about 1700, and the Mississauga move into Alnwick Township is the 1830s. This file does not invent an 11th-century nation, a meeting, a village, or a mound.

## Climate

Dry sandy hills at the cool, wet edge of what tallgrass can stand. Grass holds the sands. Trees win where the ground is moister. Summers are warm enough for big bluestem taller than a person. Winters are still cold. This is not a treeless high plain, and it is not the Saint John intervale.

The game’s night stays the coast rule: the same cold, the same hearts. Do not invent a hotter day or a grass fire that spreads. The fire in the sources is why the hills stayed open over centuries. It is not a new system.

Ground underfoot:

- **Dry sand** of the Oak Ridges Moraine, and the dark soil the grass builds. The floodplain skin of the valley is silt over clay. This is not that.
- No peat, no granite, no sphagnum, no tuckamore.
- Scattered oaks in the grass. Not a closed maple canopy. Not a fir woods.

## Fauna (must have sources)

Large game here is the eastern elk, in the forest and the openings, not a bison herd. White-tailed deer belong at the edge and are already the valley’s trail. Do not make this band a second deer park.

## Mineral, and the travel tool

The mission line is that iron is a travel tool. These sources do not support that craft on this corridor.

- **Bog iron and furnace iron are real, and they are the wrong century.** Northern Ohio’s bog ore fed charcoal furnaces from 1825 to 1860. Ohio’s first blast furnace, Hopewell Furnace near Poland, is 1804. On Lake Erie, Normandale burned charcoal and bog iron from 1815. Marmora’s works, on Blairton ore, were producing pig iron by 1823. None of that is an 11th-century road.
- **The Norse bog-iron smelt stays on the coast.** It is a Newfoundland fact. It does not move inland onto these hills.
- **The interior’s real tool stone is chert, and it is not on these hills.** Flint Ridge, in Licking and Muskingum counties, Ohio, is Vanport flint quarried for more than 12,000 years into knives, scrapers, and drills, and carried a long way. The famous long-distance trade is especially Adena and Hopewell, centuries before the 11th. The Oak Ridges Moraine under the Rice Lake Plains is sand and gravel. This pass did not find a chert ridge there. Do not slide Flint Ridge onto the savanna to satisfy the word “mineral.”
- **No craft is named.** A carried chert edge is a true tool of the wider interior. It is not, in these sources, the thing that makes this grassland walkable the way a raft makes the Saint John crossable. New Jersey tea roots as hunting fuel are a plant use from the Missouri River record, not a mineral, and not a recipe for this band.

Phase H of `docs/PLAN.md` stops on that gap. It does not invent ore, a bloomery, or a flint knife to fill the slot.

## Do not include

- **The Madawaska / Central Uplands as this biome.** Forest, not grass.
- **Shortgrass, mixed-grass high plains, and a bison horizon.** Wrong corridor.
- **Bison**, until a source in this file places them on these hills.
- **A reintroduced elk herd** as if it were the 11th-century animal. The draft animal is the extirpated eastern elk.
- **Horses, cattle, sheep.** Later.
- **Kentucky bluegrass, white sweet clover, spotted knapweed, dog-strangling vine, common buckthorn, Scotch pine.** The species booklet lists these as what replaced and invaded the remnant. Not the old cover.
- **Maize as scatter forage.** Gardens are a separate pass. This file did not establish an 11th-century cornfield on these hills.
- **Wild rice as the ground.** The English name Rice Lake is not, in the sources read here, a license to spawn `Zizania` on the sand hills. A Wisconsin “Rice Lake” manoomin record is a different lake. Do not merge them.
- **Butternut, riverbank grape, fiddlehead, fir, spruce, peat, granite, bakeapple.** Those are the coast or the valley.
- **Sugar maple as the chop.** It is the upland and valley tree this band is here to leave behind.
- **Iron ore, a bloom, a pig, a nail** as a pickup or a craft.
- **Hopewell mounds, a quarry town, a village.** The ridge’s history is not a settlement to spawn.
- Palms, and generic “prairie grass” with no species.
