Research only. Nothing in this file is authorized for `game/`, spawn tables, art filenames, or PixelLab prompts until that skim. There is no `river_country.tres` yet. The id is `river_country`, the same id the continent map already uses for this band. The ladder name stays **River country**.

This is one southbound river out of the country just south of the Maritimes. It is not a second Mississippi, and it is not the Saint John again.

## Ecoregion

- Real-world analogue: the **Connecticut River**, New England’s longest river. It leaves Fourth Connecticut Lake in Pittsburg, New Hampshire, on the Canadian border, and runs south through New Hampshire, Vermont, Massachusetts, and Connecticut to Long Island Sound. New Hampshire calls it 410 miles. The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service calls the same course more than 400 miles, joined by 148 smaller waterways, and a wide channel where it meets the sound.
- The plants in this file are the river’s floodplain and the terraces above it, in the valley through Massachusetts and Connecticut, about 42°N. They are not the spruce-fir pond at the source, and they are not the tidal marshes at the mouth. The source pond is the same spruce the coast already has. The mouth is a wetland this walk does not spawn.
- Compressed journey: in the game this band sits inland of the bayou. The walk is shortened. The miles from Louisiana back up to New England are not tiled. The plants are this river’s plants. On the real map the Connecticut is the southbound river between the Gulf of St. Lawrence and the later eastern states. The chunk order is the mission’s ladder, not a latitude sort.
- Not the Ohio, and not the Mississippi main stem. The bayou is already the Mississippi’s swamp. The Ohio is a real road west, and it sits across the Appalachians. It is not the river you take south out of the Maritimes.
- Not the Hudson. The Champlain–Hudson road does run from the St. Lawrence south, and chestnut reaches that valley too. The lower Hudson is an estuary. This pass takes the Connecticut because the long river of New England itself is a forested floodplain, not a second sea.
- Not the Penobscot or the Kennebec as this band. Those Maine rivers are still the spruce-fir transition the coast and the Saint John already cover.
- Period: 11th century. American chestnut was a canopy tree. The blight that took it out of the canopy was found in 1904 and had reached Connecticut by the 1920s. Dutch elm disease, the main-stem dams, and Japanese knotweed are later. The 11th-century river flooded. It was not a reservoir. The valley was already lived in. This file does not design a meeting or a village.

## Climate

Humid continental in the valley, milder than the mountains on either side, colder than the open valley farther south. Winters are still real. The game’s night stays the coast rule anyway: same cold, same hearts. Do not invent a warm night, a flood season, or a tide clock.

Ground underfoot, in three kinds:

- **Main stem.** The Connecticut itself. One river, not a fan of bayou channels. He does not wade it.
- **Tributaries.** The smaller waterways that join it. He can wade those, the way he wades a brook. They are not the stem.
- **Floodplain and terrace.** Cottonwood and sycamore on the ground the river floods. Chestnut and shagbark hickory on the drier ground above that flood. That terrace is the walk that is not a bank.

No peat bog, no granite shore, no cypress, no tallgrass hill.

## Flora (must have sources)

The change from the swamp is a single large river and a chestnut terrace. Do not copy fir, spruce, baldcypress, water tupelo, or big bluestem onto this band to fill it. Silver maple, American elm, ostrich fern, and riverbank grape do grow here. They are already valley species. They are not why this band exists.

## Fauna (must have sources)

No new trail animal. White-tailed deer use the floodplain as a corridor, and the valley already has the deer path. Do not start this river with another deer.

The river has migratory fish, and a modern restoration program exists for them. Shad and sturgeon are not a new catch. Fishing stays the five fish on the Saint John stem.

## Water, for the later spawn

Phase L needs the river this file actually describes. The sources support one stem and many smaller waterways, and no width in tiles.

- The **main stem** is the barrier. He does not wade it. The raft he already carries can cross it. This file does not ask for a second boat.
- A **tributary** is the smaller water. He can wade it. It is not a second stem, and it is not a place to cast.
- The **floodplain** is cottonwood and sycamore. The **terrace** is chestnut, shagbark, and the chestnut nut. He walks both. The terrace is the ground off the annual flood.
- The modern mouth is a wide channel, and the lower river is tidal. Those facts are not a tile width, a tide clock, or a dam. Phase L may use the split above. It may not invent a depth, a flood calendar, a tide, or a reservoir. It may not turn this river into the Atchafalaya.

## Do not include

- **A second Mississippi, or the Ohio, as this band.** The swamp already is the Mississippi’s distributary. The Ohio is a different corridor and was not chosen.
- **The Hudson estuary, the tidal mouth, or the spruce-fir source pond.** Wrong water, or a forest the coast already has.
- **Baldcypress, water tupelo, Spanish moss, pecan, muscadine.** The cypress range on the Atlantic coastal plain starts far south of this valley. Those are the bayou.
- **Fir, spruce, peat, granite, bakeapple, blueberry** as the look of this band. The source pond has spruce. This band does not.
- **Big bluestem, black oak savanna, New Jersey tea, elk, bison.** Those are the Rice Lake hills, or they were refused there.
- **Silver maple, elm, ostrich fern, or riverbank grape as the new species.** They belong in the valley’s list. They can stand in the mix. They are not the signature.
- **Box elder and hackberry as Connecticut trees.** The heritage account places those in the Housatonic subcanopy.
- **Chinese chestnut, a blight canker, a stump sprout as the picture of the old woods.** The 11th-century tree was the canopy chestnut.
- **A dam, a canal, a salmon hatchery, Japanese knotweed, oriental bittersweet.** Later uses and later plants.
- **A new fishery.** The hand-line’s water is the valley stem. This river does not add a fish.
- **A new boat, a tide that opens a path, or a land-bridge.** The raft is the crossing. The pinned land-bridge wish is not decided here.
